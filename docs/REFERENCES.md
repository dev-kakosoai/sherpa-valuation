**Residual value of construction and farm machinery (sale side only).**

learning on heavy-equipment resale data; Milošević, Kovačević & Petronijević (2021, *J. Constr.

Thibodeau (2003); Efron & Morris (1973); Fay & Herriot (1979); Micci-Barreca (2001, target
encoding with shrinkage); Halvorsen & Pollakowski (1981, functional form).

## What SHERPA claims as new

Within the sources searched, no published model fits the **sale prices and rental rates of used
heavy equipment jointly from marketplace listings with one shared value function**, and none
estimates a **rental duration-discount curve** for equipment from data; trade rate guides state
such multipliers as rules of thumb. SHERPA's contribution is the combination: a shared
machine-value core identified by both equations, a per-category rent-to-value level tied to the
user cost of capital, a per-category duration-discount slope, an operator effect, empirical-Bayes
pooling over a brand → origin hierarchy, and normalised conformal intervals - open source and
evaluated with ablations.

SHERPA does **not** claim to be the first hedonic model of rents or of sales, the first
user-cost price-rent model, the first conformal valuation model, or the first partially pooled
hedonic model. Any "first" for the joint equipment model should read *"to our knowledge"*.
